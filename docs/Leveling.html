The skill that compounds: remove dead time

Fast levelling is a sustained loop: choose worthwhile objectives, travel through useful kills, complete several objectives together, return once, repeat. A beautiful rotation cannot compensate for repeated empty journeys, poor quest selection or long group waits.

Optimise XP per total session hour. Include travel, recovery, deaths, selling, searching and group formation. A damage meter answers a different question. Compare similar levels and rested states; a quest-turn-in burst is not a sustainable hourly rate.

Illustration, not a benchmark: 40 productive minutes becoming 50 is a 25% increase in productive time. Making those original 40 minutes 10% faster adds only four minutes' worth of output. Fix the biggest time loss first.

Your friend's calendar progress alone cannot identify his method. The useful comparison is /played, class, route, solo versus organised group, rested state and outside help. Ask him for those and one uninterrupted 30-minute questing segment: the decisions between fights may teach you more than his build.

Your next session at 24

  1. Make one preparation stop. Check the level-24 trainer unlocks below, update your native spell ranks manually, spend available talent points, repair, empty bags and carry recovery supplies. Check whether your weapon is badly behind. Set your hearth to the hub you will actually return to.
  2. Stay near productive quests. In Wetlands, inspect the excavation, swamp and Greenwarden chains already unlocked. In Ashenvale or Duskwood, finish an accessible local cluster before crossing continents. The old reminder's zone is a candidate, not an instruction to abandon your quest log.
  3. Plan one circuit. Read objectives and prerequisites, identify overlapping kill/drop areas, and pick a return point. Avoid separate out-and-back journeys for quests sharing the same location.
  4. Run a complete 30–45 minute sample. Include the turn-ins and return journey. Start with ordinary safe Cat pulls. Record the largest interruption rather than changing five things at once.
  5. Change one bottleneck next loop. Too much drinking: spend less mana per pull. Too much walking: batch better. Contested spawns: move. Slow kills: check weapon, trained ranks, mob difficulty and ability choices.
  6. End ready to play. Sell and restock together; log out in a verified rest location. Leave yourself a one-line next objective so the next session starts immediately.

For a short session, a nearby complete loop beats spending half the session travelling to a theoretically superior zone. For a long session, a worthwhile relocation has more time to repay its cost.

Mana is your recovery and safety budget

Heal enough to continue safely, then return to sustained form combat. Avoid tiny repeated top-ups and unnecessary form changes. Refresh useful buffs during a heal/vendor stop when practical. If Shifting Power is learned, extra energy can help, but spending your last healing/escape mana to win a trivial kill is a poor trade. On a PvP realm, leave more reserve than an isolated PvE damage test suggests.

When pulling becomes unreliable, choose easier enemies or a less crowded area. A higher-level mob's nominal XP is not automatically better once misses, recovery and deaths are counted. Keep your camera useful, avoid fighting into respawns, and move on after repeated ganks rather than feeding corpse runs.

Two-minute audit: are bleeds expiring on living targets or dying half-used? Are you waiting for five points on a nearly dead mob? Drinking after every few kills? Standing still deciding the next target? Those observations tell you what to fix.

Plan beyond 30

Continue a maintained 30–60 route rather than extrapolating the old Classic checklist. Blizzard describes more than a thousand added quests, including Riverglades content spanning much of the mid-30s to mid-40s. Treat that as a deliberate route option, not content to ignore because a vanilla guide skips it. Hyjal is another new region; use a current route for its entry requirements and ordering. Blizzard's world overview.

At each new hub, repeat the same audit: accessible objectives, total circuit time, competition, useful rewards, onward travel. At later levels, group quests and dungeon prerequisites become planning decisions: collect compatible ones before the run and avoid a second trip caused by skipping the prerequisite.

Choose or skip a quest

  • Take: overlapping objectives, dense accessible kills, short hand-ins, a useful unlock, or a substantial equipment reward on your route.
  • Defer: an isolated delivery, an elite without a ready group, a heavily contested named spawn, or a poor-drop-rate objective that has stalled your loop.
  • Check first: the next stage. A weak first step can unlock a strong cluster; a tempting reward may hide a long chain.

Kill useful, efficient mobs along the path when that advances objectives or fills a short XP gap. Do not turn a good travel route into an indiscriminate grind of everything in sight. Never stay solely because you have already invested time: compare the next ten minutes.

Dungeon, duo or solo?

A prepared dungeon group can be excellent; waiting for one earns nothing. Keep questing while forming. Check your role, relevant quests/prerequisites, travel, likely completion time and the group's ability to finish. Feral's tank option can reduce formation time, but only count it as an advantage if you can handle that dungeon with the available gear and healer.

For a first clear, include quest rewards. For repeats, remove those one-time rewards from the estimate. Include wipes, replacements, selling, resets and any applicable instance-entry limits. A repeat run must beat your sustainable solo loop, not an artificially bad five-minute sample.

Verified anti-boost rule: Blizzard's 15 November update says dungeon enemy XP is nullified when a group member is more than 12 levels above that enemy. This is relative to the enemy, not simply your own level or the dungeon's advertised bracket. A level-60 carry is therefore not a reliable XP plan. Official explanation.

A duo can reduce danger, allow rear attacks and finish group objectives. It can also create waiting and shared-credit/drop bottlenecks. Compare each player's XP across the entire loop. Agree on the route and pace before starting; use help for difficult objectives and logistics rather than assuming grouping always accelerates levelling.

Detours must pay for themselves

If your normal route yields 30,000 XP/hour, a 12-minute detour costs roughly 6,000 XP of alternative progress. A dungeon, weapon quest or training trip may still be worth it through its own XP and future time saved. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a promised rate. Evaluate the whole remaining session, not only the immediate reward.

Rest and buffs: use them without serving them

Arrange necessary breaks and logout around rest opportunities. Check the current buff tooltip: rested XP, an all-XP multiplier and a combat-stat buff are different benefits. Do not assume a bonus affects quest hand-ins unless it says so. Camping can supply buffs; using an available camp during a break is different from spending your play session hunting the perfect one.

The sleeping-bag guide reports up to 3% extra XP after three minutes, lasting two hours, with a travel-heavy acquisition chain. Its Student Fodder description is health recovery, not the old Season of Discovery rested-XP consumable. Check your actual item. Neryssa's sleeping-bag guide (10 October; pre-launch, reconfirm tooltip).

Break-even example: a pure 3% XP bonus must cover about 100 productive minutes to repay three otherwise productive minutes spent acquiring that activation: 3 ÷ 0.03. A ten-minute detour takes about 333 minutes of bonus uptime. If you were going AFK anyway, that activation time has little opportunity cost. Acquisition quests, useful rewards and rest benefits require their own accounting; do not count the same benefit twice.

Use convenient food and useful consumables when their cost is justified. No unverified stacking percentage or blanket “collect every XP buff first” recommendation belongs in a speed plan.

Druid and Herbalism

Movement forms make travel between nodes convenient. That does not make harvesting passive, and it does not make self-gathered herbs free. Travel Form arrives at 30. I have not verified Forever-specific herb interaction while transformed; do not base the decision on Retail flight-form gathering behavior.

A small, repeatable Alchemy routine

  1. Choose two or three recipes you can already make: check mana potions and useful elixirs first.
  2. Compare finished-item sale prices with the full cost of herbs, vials and other reagents. Value your gathered herbs at their alternative sale value.
  3. Make one small trial batch, for example 5–10 items, only when the margin is worthwhile. That is a trial size, not a sales forecast.
  4. List once, then go play. Check sold mail next session. Avoid an endless cancel-and-relist routine.
  5. Repeat what actually sells. If a craft loses money, sell the herbs or wait; do not craft merely because the recipe is available.

Gather only when it is convenient. Buying reasonably priced herbs is compatible with being an Herbalist. At level 24, preserving training money matters more than rushing profession skill.

Keep professions secondary to your current objective. Gather herbs along the quest route; batch crafting and auction-house work at the end of a session. Check current trainer recipes before investing in skill-ups.

Self-crafted gear: count materials, recipes, skill-up costs and time. Crafting can be economical, but your own materials are still valuable. Buy selective tradable upgrades; choose a gear craft for a specific reward you actually want.

Is this batch worth making?

Enter all prices in the same unit (gold or silver). This estimates margin if the entire batch sells; it does not predict demand or include initial profession leveling costs.

Deposits returned on successful sales are not a final expense; include deposits actually lost to failed listings or cancellation. If you gather, include that time in active minutes.
